A pricing platform, disguised as a hiring platform.

FairFix opens with the number a homeowner actually wants: a fair estimate before any tradesman is even chosen. Five screens carry them from that first tap to a paid inspection — inspired by what makes Khadmaty easy to scan, redrawn for the Irish market it will actually live in.

Job of the screen

Prove trust and speed in the first fold.

The header opens with location (Eircode-aware) and greeting; the AI banner sits directly under search so the platform's one true differentiator lands before scroll.

Deliberate choices
  • Copy is Irish English: "Good morning" not "Hi", "trade" not "profession".
  • Trust row uses local proof points (RECI, Google, public liability) — not made-up star badges.
  • AI banner has a "TRY" chip, not a big button — invites, doesn't shove.
  • Bottom nav is four items, not five. Five is the app-store cliché; four scans faster.
What we borrowed from Khadmaty

The 4-column tile grid and sectioned categories. What we replaced: the illustration-heavy tiles (visual noise in English UI), the red-first palette (semantically "danger" in Ireland), and the RTL header.

Job of the screen

Convert vague intent into a specific service.

The homeowner rarely knows the label a tradesman uses. Each sub-service pairs the trade name with plain-English description (Sockets, tripping, no power) so people self-diagnose without help.

Deliberate choices
  • Every row shows a typical price band — sets expectations before the AI even runs.
  • Emergency call-out is coral-tinted and pinned to the top. Real emergencies convert best when they're one tap.
  • SEAI grant callout on EV charger is a wedge — Ireland's most-searched electrician service in 2026.
  • No stock illustrations. Icons only. Faster to scan, cheaper to produce, no copyright risk.
Why not Khadmaty's grid again?

Grid is right for shallow browsing (home). Once the user has picked a trade, a list is right — it lets each row hold price context and a description without cramping.

Job of the screen

Turn a photo + a sentence into a fair number.

This is the whole differentiator. The AI never says what is wrong (that would create liability) — it only shows the price band similar jobs settled at. The homeowner then sets their own offer, anchored on that band.

Deliberate choices
  • Live confidence score (82%). Undersells the AI a little, which builds trust rather than promising perfection.
  • The estimate is a band, never a single number. Single numbers get read as promises.
  • Slider defaults to just below the midpoint — behavioural nudge; tradesmen still get a fair offer.
  • The CTA copy names who receives it ("nearby electricians") — removes the mystery of what happens next.
Compliance guardrails

Never generate diagnosis text. Never quote parts. Cache no image beyond 30 days. Every AI response logged for admin review (per your spec).

Job of the screen

Make choosing a person feel low-stakes.

Offers arrive over minutes; the header ("3 offers in 4 minutes") makes the wait feel like momentum, not delay. One "Top Match" is highlighted so decision-fatigued homeowners get a default without losing choice.

Deliberate choices
  • Counter-offers show the tradesman's reason in their own words. This is the platform's answer to Bark's biggest complaint: no context, just prices.
  • Verification badges use Ireland's real credentials (RECI, RGII, Safe Pass) — cheap trust, high signal.
  • Distance in km, not miles. Response time in minutes, not "fast". Specifics build credibility.
  • "New this month" badge protects new tradesmen from the cold-start rating penalty that kills most marketplaces.
What's under the hood

Sort weights: distance × 0.35, rating × 0.30, response time × 0.20, price fit × 0.15. All tunable from admin per your spec. Push notifies homeowner on first three offers only — after that, in-app only.

Job of the screen

Trade money for certainty, in one screen.

The €15 inspection fee is the platform's defence against no-shows on both sides. Escrow copy is direct — "held", "releases when he arrives", "you get it back" — no legal fog.

Deliberate choices
  • Apple Pay is default on iOS; Revolut Card second. Both are how Irish millennials actually pay.
  • Cash option is present but +€2. Cash-at-door still matters in rural Ireland; the surcharge covers admin without banning it.
  • Full breakdown (job price €120 + inspection €15) makes the total transparent. No hidden platform fee — commission comes from the tradesman side.
  • GDPR line at the bottom, factual, no dark pattern. Frankfurt data centre satisfies EU data residency by default.
After confirm

Push to tradesman ("Job booked — leave now"), calendar entry auto-created for both, in-app chat unlocks, live location shared 30 min before ETA. Order tracking screen (out of MVP scope for these five).

What holds it together

A small, chosen palette; two typefaces earning their spots; and four rules the whole app defers to. Everything else falls out of that.

Palette
Ink · Navy#0F1E2E
Ground · Paper#FAF7F2
Accent · Emerald#0E9F6E
Highlight · Amber#C88A2A
Signal · Coral#C4573F

Navy earns trust the way Irish banks do; emerald is our accent — a subtle nod, not a stereotype. Coral is the only red on the app — reserved for emergencies. Paper (warm off-white) replaces sterile #FFF: reads as considered on the shelf next to competitors.

Old Style serif for anything that carries voice — headlines, prices, tradesman names. System sans for everything scanned. Mono for anything measured. No third face.

Rules
R1
Numbers earn typography.
Every price, rating, distance and confidence score uses the display serif. Nothing else in the interface does — so numbers always feel like answers.
R2
One accent per screen.
Emerald marks progress and confirmation only. Coral marks emergency only. Amber marks earned attention (a counter, a rating). If everything glows, nothing does.
R3
Trust is specific.
Never "verified" — always "RECI verified". Never "fast" — always "replied 3 min". Real certificates and real numbers beat any badge design.
R4
The AI shows its work.
Always a band, not a number. Always with a confidence score. Always with a sample size ("340 similar jobs"). Never a diagnosis.
